I am a Ph.D. student in Economics and Finance at Hanyang University. I am currently applying to Ph.D. programs in Economics in the United States and Canada, with research interests in Macroeconomics, Monetary Economics, and International Economics.

My research combines structural macroeconomic modeling with large-scale administrative microdata. My primary working paper, which serves as my Ph.D. application writing sample, uses an administrative firm-level panel of over 950,000 firm-years to study how exchange rate volatility affects the return on invested capital of Korean exporters, documenting a U-shaped relationship driven by the option value of exporting and the dollar fixed cost. In a second project, joint with Prof. Daeha Cho, I study how quasi-hyperbolic discounting affects the transmission of forward guidance in a New Keynesian model.

Before starting my Ph.D., I completed my M.A. (with the Best Dissertation Award, Hanyang University) and B.A. (Cum Laude) in Economics and Finance at Hanyang University, including a period of military service in the Republic of Korea Army.
