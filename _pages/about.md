I am a Ph.D. student in Economics and Finance at Hanyang University, working under the supervision of Prof. Daeha Cho. I am currently on the job market for U.S. Ph.D. programs in Economics, with research interests in Macroeconomics, Monetary Economics, and Applied Macroeconometrics.

My research combines structural macroeconomic modeling with large-scale administrative microdata. In my job market paper, I use an administrative firm-level panel of over 971,000 firm-years to study how exchange-rate volatility affects the return on invested capital of Korean exporters, documenting a U-shaped relationship driven by the option value of exporting. In a second project, joint with Prof. Daeha Cho, I study how quasi-hyperbolic discounting affects the transmission of forward guidance in a New Keynesian model.

Before starting my Ph.D., I completed my M.A. (with the Best Dissertation Award, Hanyang University) and B.A. (Cum Laude) in Economics and Finance at Hanyang University, including a period of military service in the Republic of Korea Army.
